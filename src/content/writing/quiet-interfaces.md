A quiet interface is often mistaken for an empty one. But quietness is not a lack of decisions. It is what remains after many decisions have been made well.

## Hierarchy before decoration

When type size, measure, and spacing explain a page clearly, decoration can become a supporting voice. One accent color is enough. A border can do the work of a shadow. Whitespace can establish a relationship more naturally than another container.

The aim is not minimalism as an aesthetic pose. The aim is to reduce the distance between a reader and what they came to understand.

## Calm still needs feedback

Restraint should never make an interface inert. Links need a visible focus state. A title can shift color. An arrow can move four pixels. These signals are small, but they make the surface feel responsive and trustworthy.

> A good interaction confirms intent without interrupting it.

## Remove until the structure appears

The useful question during a visual review is not “what else can go here?” It is “what can disappear without losing meaning?” Often the result is not merely simpler. It is more specific, because the content finally has enough room to establish its own character.
